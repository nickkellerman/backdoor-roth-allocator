Good to know

Backdoor Roth checklist and common pitfalls
  1. Contribute to a traditional IRA as a non-deductible contribution, up to $7,500 for 2026 ($8,600 if 50+). Contributions for a tax year can be made until the tax-filing deadline the following April.
  2. Let it settle as cash. Many people leave it in the settlement/money-market fund rather than investing before converting.
  3. Convert the traditional IRA balance to your Roth IRA.
  4. Invest it inside the Roth. That's what this tool helps with.
  5. File Form 8606 with your taxes to report the non-deductible contribution and the conversion.

Pro-rata rule (the big one). If you have any pre-tax money in a traditional, SEP or SIMPLE IRA on December 31 of the year you convert, part of your conversion becomes taxable. Money in a 401(k) or 403(b) doesn't count. Rolling pre-tax IRA money into a workplace 401(k) before year-end is a common fix.

Quick pro-rata check: pre-tax IRA balance you expect on Dec 31
  • Interest before converting: any interest earned while the money waits in the traditional IRA is taxable when converted, but it's usually only a few dollars.
  • Why use the backdoor: you'd only need it if your income is too high to contribute to a Roth directly. Check IRS.gov for this year's income limits.
  • Spouses can each do their own backdoor Roth, including through a spousal IRA.
  • Mega backdoor Roth: some 401(k) plans let you make after-tax contributions and convert them, which allows much larger amounts.
Real-world things to watch when you place the trades
  • Whole vs. fractional shares: not every brokerage supports fractional ETF shares. If yours doesn't, round down to whole shares and leave the rest as cash (or add it to your largest buy).
  • Mutual fund minimums: some funds need $1,000–$3,000 for a first purchase. You can't add a small amount to a fund you don't own yet.
  • Prices move: this tool works in dollars. Use a limit order or "buy in dollars" if your broker supports it.
  • Settlement: converted money may take a day or two to become available to trade.
  • Overlapping funds: VTI, VOO, FXAIX and SWTSX are all mostly US large-company stocks. Use "By asset class" so overlapping funds count together.
  • Small amounts, big accounts: once your Roth is large, one year's contribution can only nudge the mix. The "conversions to reach target" estimate shows how long new money alone would take.
Privacy and how this tool handles your data
  • No accounts, no server, no database, no analytics, no cookies.
  • Everything runs in your browser. The page's security policy blocks it from sending your data anywhere.
  • Screenshots are read on your device with the open-source Tesseract text reader. The reader's code is downloaded the first time you import an image. Your image is never uploaded, and it's discarded right after reading.
  • The text read from screenshots, CSVs and pasted text is filtered: only tickers, fund names and dollar amounts are kept. Long number strings (like account numbers), masked account numbers and email addresses are dropped, and the raw text is never shown or stored.
  • "Remember" (off by default) saves only tickers, fund names, asset classes, targets and the amount to invest, and only in this browser. Unchecking it erases them.
